# Social storytelling

- Select one central takeaway that serves the campaign goal and target reader. Make the opening concrete enough to earn attention without a fabricated result or exaggerated promise.
- For short video, carry that idea from an immediate visual or spoken hook through a sequence of observable moments, then a fitting next step. Make shots and timing practical for the available assets.
- For image-text, let the cover introduce the idea and give each following image a distinct role in explaining, demonstrating, or resolving it. Align body copy with the image sequence.
- Vary headline options by legitimate reader angle, such as a recognizable situation, a useful explanation, a supported benefit, or a real story. Avoid repeating one template with synonyms.
- Write for a person on the selected platform: use the audience's language, concrete details, and a natural invitation to respond or act. Avoid engagement bait and generic marketing slogans.
- Follow the host task's output structure, content format, language, and hashtag policy. Do not infer fixed posting schedules or algorithm rules from this skill.
